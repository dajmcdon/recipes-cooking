Instant pot beef chili

soup
instantpot
Published

October 25, 2025

Adapted from Serious Eats, Daniel Gritzer

Note that measurements for the various spices are approximate. I usually just dump some in and adjust later if it doesn’t taste quite right.

There are 4 flavour components. I use whatever I have. So sometimes I omit the tomato paste or the chocolate, and compensate with extra coffee or fish sauce or soy sauce.

  • 1 lb dried red kidney beans (or 2 large cans, in which case, skip to step 2.)
  • 1 onion
  • 1 carrot
  • 1 clove garlic
  • 1 celery stalk
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 lbs ground beef
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 1 28oz can diced tomatoes
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 T chili powder
  • 1/2 t onion powder
  • 1/2 t garlic powder
  • 1/2 t ground cumin
  • cayenne (optional)
  • 1/3 can blended chipotles in adobo (put the rest in a freezer bag for later)
  • 1 T tomato paste
  • 3 T leftover (strong) coffee
  • 1/2 oz dark, unsweetened, chocolate (or baking chocolate)
  • 1 t fish sauce or Worcestershire sauce

Sour cream, shredded cheese, sliced radishes, cubed avocado, crushed tortilla chips, sliced scallions, diced onions, lime wedges, or pickled jalapeño, for garnishes.

  1. If using dried beans, soak them overnight in salted water (should taste like soup, not like water or the ocean) along with 1/4 tsp baking soda. Drain, add the beans along with more salt, the onion, garlic, carrot, celery, and 1 bay leaf to the pressure cooker. Cover with water so that there’s about 2 inches of water above the top of the beans. Seal the pressure cooker, and cook on high pressure for 20 minutes. Either quick-release or not, depending on when you need the beans. Drain, reserving 1-2 cups liquid. Clean the cooker.

  2. Add a bit of oil to the bottom of the cooker, and set to saute on medium. Brown about 1/4 of the ground beef (the temp needs to be high enough that it sizzles, not just turns grey). Season with salt and pepper. Once browned, add the onion and saute about 5 minutes. Then add the garlic for another minute. Turn off cooker.

  3. Add remaining ingredients (including the rest of the beef), but not the beans. Stir well, being sure to scrape the browned bits off the bottom. It should be thick, but there should be enough liquid that it doesn’t appear dry. If it does, add a little bean liquid, water to rinse the tomato can, or just some water. Probably no more than 1/2 cup. Add some salt. You can’t taste at this point, so better to under season.

  4. Seal the pressure cooker, and cook on high for 20 minutes. Quick-release the pressure. Open and stir well. Taste for seasoning, adding more spices or fish sauce, or salt. Add the drained beans and saute to warm them through. If it’s still too liquidy, saute on high for a few minutes. If it’s too dry, add some bean water or water. Serve with toppings.
